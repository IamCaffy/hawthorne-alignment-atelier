The eighteen classifications

Each point is a distinct descriptive classification, not merely a subdivision of the traditional nine alignments. These descriptions are the Atelier's fixed reference definitions.

Lawfulness · 1 to 9

  1. 1
    Unrestrained

    Governed primarily by immediate desires and circumstances, with little commitment to consistent principles.

  2. 2
    Impulsive

    Personal desires routinely override previously established beliefs and commitments.

  3. 3
    Inconsistent

    Has recognizable principles but frequently abandons them when inconvenient.

  4. 4
    Flexible

    Maintains principles but readily makes exceptions based on changing circumstances.

  5. 5
    Balanced

    Gives relatively equal weight to personal principles and situational judgment.

  6. 6
    Dependable

    Generally follows established principles, making exceptions when sufficiently justified.

  7. 7
    Disciplined

    Has a well-established personal code and follows it even when inconvenient.

  8. 8
    Unwavering

    Rarely compromises personal principles, even when substantial sacrifices are required.

  9. 9
    Absolute

    Follows a clearly defined personal code regardless of personal consequences, temptation, or external pressure.

Goodness · 1 to 9

  1. 1
    Malevolent

    Deliberately seeks to cause harm, suffering, or destruction.

  2. 2
    Cruel

    Readily harms, manipulates, or exploits others with little remorse.

  3. 3
    Exploitative

    Consistently prioritizes personal gain over others’ welfare even when significant harm results.

  4. 4
    Self-Serving

    Primarily concerned with personal interests but generally avoids unnecessary harm.

  5. 5
    Indifferent

    Neither consistently prioritizes nor disregards the well-being of others.

  6. 6
    Considerate

    Generally respects others’ welfare and helps when personal costs are reasonable.

  7. 7
    Compassionate

    Actively helps others and prevents suffering, even when meaningful personal sacrifice is required.

  8. 8
    Altruistic

    Consistently prioritizes others’ welfare and willingly accepts considerable personal sacrifice.

  9. 9
    Selfless

    Demonstrates extraordinary commitment to others’ well-being, even at tremendous personal cost.

How the traditional nine alignments fit

Lawfulness bands

1–3: Chaotic
4–6: Neutral
7–9: Lawful

Goodness bands

1–3: Evil
4–6: Neutral
7–9: Good

For example, (4, 7) means Flexible / Compassionate, traditionally Neutral Good. A (8, 3) character is Unwavering / Exploitative, traditionally Lawful Evil. Following a consistent code says nothing by itself about whether that code is kind.

Using the framework to deepen roleplay

  1. Start with who your character is. Consider their personality, ideals, flaws, bonds, backstory, and actual choices during adventures.
  2. Look at both dimensions separately. Commitment to a personal code is not the same as obedience to authority. Caring deeply about close friends is not automatically the same as putting strangers first.
  3. Consider hard choices. What happens when a promise conflicts with compassion? When a reward conflicts with someone else's safety? Do they follow a stable hierarchy of principles?
  4. Choose a provisional coordinate. Let decisions in actual play confirm or change it. A single regretted action can matter without defining a character forever.
  5. Use it as a roleplay guide, not a constraint. Alignment describes a character's current tendencies. The player decides what the character does next.
